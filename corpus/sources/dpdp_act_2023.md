# Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (India)

## Section 1 — Short Title and Commencement

This Act may be called the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023. It shall come into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, appoint.

## Section 2 — Definitions

In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires:
(a) "consent manager" means a person registered with the Board;
(b) "data fiduciary" means any person who alone or in conjunction with other persons determines the purpose and means of processing personal data;
(c) "data principal" means the individual to whom the personal data relates;
(d) "data processor" means any person who processes personal data on behalf of a data fiduciary;
(e) "personal data" means any data about an individual who is identifiable by or in relation to such data.

## Section 4 — Grounds for Processing Personal Data

A data fiduciary may process the personal data of a data principal only in accordance with the provisions of this Act and for a lawful purpose:
(a) for which the data principal has given her consent; or
(b) for certain legitimate uses specified under this Act.
Personal data shall only be processed for the purpose for which it was collected (purpose limitation).

## Section 5 — Notice

Before requesting consent, the data fiduciary shall give the data principal a notice containing:
(a) a description of personal data sought to be collected and the purpose of processing such data;
(b) the manner in which the data principal may exercise her rights under this Act;
(c) the manner in which the data principal may make a complaint to the Board.

## Section 6 — Consent

Consent of the data principal shall be free, specific, informed, unconditional, and unambiguous with a clear affirmative action. Consent may be withdrawn at any time. The burden of proof that consent was given shall lie on the data fiduciary.

## Section 7 — Certain Legitimate Uses

A data fiduciary may process personal data of a data principal without consent where such processing is necessary:
(a) for performance of any function of the State;
(b) for compliance with any law or judgment of a court;
(c) for responding to a medical emergency involving a threat to life.

## Section 8 — General Obligations of Data Fiduciary

Every data fiduciary shall:
(a) make reasonable efforts to ensure accuracy and completeness of personal data;
(b) implement appropriate technical and organisational measures to ensure effective observance of this Act;
(c) implement reasonable security safeguards to prevent personal data breach.

## Section 9 — Processing of Personal Data of Children

A data fiduciary shall, before processing any personal data of a child, obtain verifiable consent of the parent or lawful guardian. No data fiduciary shall process personal data of a child in a manner that is detrimental to the well-being of the child.

## Section 11 — Right of the Data Principal

A data principal shall have the right to:
(a) obtain information about processing of personal data;
(b) correction, completion, updating, and erasure of personal data;
(c) grievance redressal;
(d) nominate another individual to exercise rights in the event of death or incapacity.

## Section 17 — Significant Data Fiduciary

The Central Government may notify any data fiduciary or class of data fiduciaries as a Significant Data Fiduciary based on volume and sensitivity of personal data processed.

## Section 33 — Penalties

The Board may impose financial penalties for breach of this Act:
(a) failure to take security safeguards: up to Rs 250 crore;
(b) failure to notify the Board of data breach: up to Rs 200 crore;
(c) breach of obligations relating to children: up to Rs 200 crore;
(d) breach of additional obligations of Significant Data Fiduciaries: up to Rs 150 crore.
